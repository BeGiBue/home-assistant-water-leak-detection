# F04 V3: Burst, independent learning and external leak sensors

This feature implements the approved V3 contract in Issue #13. The release version
remains **1.1.0**; this branch is for independent security review, not a release.
F02 static High and F08 Low stability retain their detector rules.

## Burst and calibration

The default configured Burst base for new or missing settings is **3,500 L/h**.
Explicitly saved values, including 2,000 L/h, remain unchanged. Select a base for
the actual installation, with a safety margin above legitimate maximum use.
Two powerful consumers together or a 3/4-inch hose can be legitimate high usage;
pipe size and static pressure alone cannot establish a safe universal boundary.
As a starting calibration, allow at least 20% above the measured legitimate
maximum and test actual use. A 3,500 L/h default cannot guarantee no false alarms.
A leak and normal usage with identical flow curves cannot be distinguished from
flow evidence alone. Independent external wet sensors provide additional evidence.

```
rolling_candidate = existing safe rolling/manual adaptive candidate
long_term_candidate = confirmed_long_term_normal_max_lph * 1.20
effective_burst = max(configured_burst_base, rolling_candidate, long_term_candidate)
```

The existing rolling/manual reference, Confidence weighting, multiplier (1.8)
and heuristic hydraulic ceiling retain their meaning. The ceiling limits that
channel, **not** the independently confirmed Long-Term candidate. High remains
exactly the configured static threshold (default 600 L/h): 45 minutes OR 500 L,
with reset after five confirmed minutes strictly below 100 L/h by default.

Normal Burst requires **30 seconds of confirmed evidence** at or above the
current effective Burst boundary. Rapid Rise accelerates this same above-boundary
candidate when the positive rise is at least **1,000 L/h per 10 seconds**; its
separate **10-second** confirmation starts at the qualifying report. It never
backdates time and never prolongs a normal deadline already running. The earlier
valid deadline wins. Dropping below the boundary abandons unconfirmed monitoring.
A genuine evidence gap, a relevant Burst option change or changed effective
boundary restarts unconfirmed monitoring; an ACTIVE event keeps its physical
reset rules. Default ACTIVE reset remains 60 seconds below 500 L/h.
High bypass suppresses neither Burst nor external sensors.

## Two independent learning channels

**Rolling Learning** keeps the existing default 30-day `learning_window_days`,
P95, recent/longer reference weighting and `insufficient`, `learning`, `reliable`
Confidence. Only clean ordinary completed consumption episodes enter that pool.
High MONITORING remains suspicious there. High peaks never enter Rolling samples.

**Long-Term Learning** tracks clean completed High-MONITORING episodes separately.
One or two independent episodes do not raise a reference. First confirmation
requires three independent episodes whose peaks satisfy the inclusive rule:

```
max_peak <= min_peak * 1.15
```

Every admitted peak must be strictly **below the configured Burst base**, not
below its adaptive effective threshold. After first confirmation, each increase
requires **three new** independent pending episodes above the previous maximum.
Old confirmed episodes cannot provide those three votes. A fourth lower or equal
peak cannot increase the reference. This admission anchor prevents a self-feeding
ratchet through an automatically rising effective threshold.

Pending and confirmed audit records do not expire with days or years. Their
original completion timestamps, episode identities, source generation and
learning epoch are retained. Both lists and authorized future records share a
4096-record budget: validate all input, reject conflicting episode identities,
deduplicate, sort deterministically and retain the newest records. The separately
validated confirmed maximum remains monotonic even when audit records are evicted.
It never depends on the presence of an old Rolling sample.

High ACTIVE, any other ACTIVE alarm, Burst/Rapid-Rise candidates, High bypass,
unknown/unavailable/invalid flow, genuine excluded evidence gaps, source rebind
and restart exclude an episode. A running episode is not persisted. After every
startup, High learning is blocked until the full configured physical High quiet
reset is confirmed after startup. A brief zero or sub-High report cannot release
this guard. Internal ticks supply no measurement evidence. The existing absolute
2-microsecond evidence tolerance remains unchanged.

F15 retains exact per-record and per-list future permissions after a bounded
clock rollback. Future records cannot confirm a cluster before UTC catches up;
they are checked again against the fixed configured base. A large rollback may
discard individual future records but does not invalidate a legitimate confirmed
summary. Rolling F15 and Confidence remain independent.

`reset_learning` clears both channels and rollback context and creates a new
learning epoch. An actual flow-source replacement changes the source generation
and clears learning; a proven registry rename preserves generation. A source
without registry provenance cannot prove learning continuity across restart.

## Upgrade from the shared 1.1.0 learner

Runtime HA Store major version **2** and learning schema **2** quarantine legacy
`samples`, `pending_high_candidates` and `confirmed_high_history` without guessing
their origin. Both new channels start fresh. Only diagnostic migration ID, time,
count and quarantine marker are retained; legacy peaks never re-enter learning.
The marker and empty channels are saved in one atomic HA Store write. Retry is
idempotent, including after a interrupted migration. Configuration, thresholds,
recipients, ACK, ACTIVE hydraulic alarms, Shutoff contributions, bypass and
hydraulic settings are preserved. A failed write blocks learning and external
reset until a safety snapshot is durably committed.

A real legacy-learning migration creates one persistent HA Repair warning with
a stable per-entry ID. New/empty installations produce no warning. Dismissal is
preserved. Losing previous adaptive learning may **lower** the effective Burst
threshold; check calibration after upgrade. Back up the HA configuration first.
A corrupt unreadable runtime Store cannot be reconstructed safely; restore a
backup rather than inventing prior alarm/learning state.

## External sources and configuration

In **Settings → Devices & services → Water Leak Guard → Configure**, open
**External leak sensors**. Add several `binary_sensor` entities, give each a name,
and configure its Shutoff contribution (default true). The menu lists configured
sources and their current lifecycle. Edit/remove, notification recipients and
expert settings have Back navigation. Initial setup can also add external sensors.
No YAML helper or separate integration is required.

| State | Evidence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| ARMED | ON | ACTIVE, fresh event ID and reset nonce |
| ACTIVE | ON / unknown / unavailable | remain ACTIVE |
| ACTIVE | OFF | ARMED, regular event end |
| ACTIVE | authorized External Reset, durably saved | RESET_WAITING_FOR_OFF |
| RESET_WAITING_FOR_OFF | ON / unknown / unavailable | remain WAITING |
| RESET_WAITING_FOR_OFF | OFF | ARMED |

Each source is independent and persists its lifecycle. A reset faulty sensor
cannot retrigger while continuously ON, including across HA restart. Externals
are independent evidence, not synthetic flow reports. Any external ACTIVE blocks
both learning channels even when its Shutoff contribution is false. WAITING alone
after successful reset does not block learning; previously tainted episodes remain
excluded and other ACTIVE alarms still block it.

Registry rename preserves source generation. Hardware replacement or remove/add
creates a new generation that cannot inherit ACTIVE/WAITING, event IDs or nonces.
Configured ACTIVE removal/replacement is rejected until OFF or authorized reset.
WAITING removal is allowed. Forced replacement keeps an old ACTIVE alarm as a
separate generation-bound tombstone; an OFF from new hardware cannot end it.
That old event can be separately authorized for reset.

## Mute, ACK and External Reset

Mute affects one recipient. Global ACK affects event notifications. Neither ends
any physical alarm or Shutoff request. `allow_global_ack` and
`allow_external_reset` are independent per-recipient permissions; External Reset
is **false by default** and requires the recipient's current tracker state
exactly `home`. `trusted_stationary` alone is insufficient.

A mobile action or `water_leak_detection.external_reset` must bind the current
entry, source, source generation, event, recipient, recipient token, action kind
and event-specific reset nonce. Replays or stale bindings are rejected. ACTIVE
nonces rotate after restart. Existing ACK/mute are preserved; use
`water_leak_detection.request_external_reset_action` to request a fresh Home-
authorized action, without cancelling ACK. Specify source generation and event ID
when multiple generations of the same source are active. Those optional fields
select the event; they do not replace the complete authorization of the reset.

All safety transitions and Store writes share a serialized writer. The reset is
staged privately; readers continue to see old ACTIVE and Shutoff until the atomic
HA file write succeeds. A failing write publishes no alarm end, no success and no
Shutoff release. Concurrent OFF/ON, options, saves and actions cannot commit over
one another. Only the exact authorized source loses its contribution. This
integration **never opens or closes a physical valve**; physical actuation remains
in explicitly configured user automations.

## Home Assistant diagnostics and limits

Existing Status, Leak alarm and Water shutoff request aggregate hydraulic and
external alarms independently of display priority. Diagnostic attributes list
all hydraulic alarms, external ACTIVE/WAITING sources, individual Shutoff
contributions and migration status. A Long-Term maximum diagnostic sensor shows
the confirmed reference; effective Burst diagnostics expose both channels.

Testing cannot prove absence of water damage or all real-world false alarms.
Correct source configuration, tracker accuracy, calibration, reliable disk writes
and user valve automations remain necessary. A defective sensor reset deliberately
removes its evidence while WAITING; other detectors do not become proof that the
physical location is dry. No merge or release authorization follows from tests.
